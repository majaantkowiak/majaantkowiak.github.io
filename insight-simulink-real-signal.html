From Simulink to a Real Injector Signal

A simulation can reproduce a convincing waveform, but visual similarity alone is not enough. For a model to become useful in engineering research, its behaviour has to be compared with measurements obtained from the real physical system.

01 / Physical model

The waveform begins with the physical system

An electromagnetic fuel injector is not only an electrical coil. Its response results from interactions between the electrical circuit, magnetic field, mechanical needle motion and hydraulic fuel flow.

To reproduce the injector current correctly, the model should therefore represent the physical mechanisms that influence current rise, needle opening, the hold phase and switch-off.

02 / Simulation

The model becomes a simulated signal

Once the physical relationships are implemented in MATLAB/Simulink and Simscape, the model can reproduce the dynamic injector response under a defined control strategy.

For an electromagnetic injector operated using Peak & Hold control, the simulated current should reproduce the main characteristic phases observed in a real injection event: rapid current increase, opening transition, regulated hold and switch-off.

03 / Experiment

Then comes the real measurement

The laboratory signal provides a reference that the numerical model cannot create on its own. It contains the real response of the injector together with effects that may be simplified or omitted in the mathematical representation.

Measurement therefore provides the link between theoretical assumptions and actual injector behaviour.

Current

Electrical response during a complete injection event.

Timing

Location and duration of characteristic waveform phases.

Repeatability

Comparison of repeated injections under similar conditions.

04 / Comparison

Overlaying the signals reveals what the model gets right

One of the simplest and most informative validation steps is to compare the simulated and measured current waveforms on the same time axis.

Agreement in overall shape is useful, but a closer comparison should also consider characteristic amplitudes, timing, transition behaviour and phase duration.

05 / Validation

Similar shape does not mean perfect model

A model may reproduce the general Peak & Hold waveform while still showing differences in local signal features. Those differences are important because they indicate where assumptions, parameters or physical relationships may require refinement.

Validation is therefore not only a final check. It is part of the model-development process.

06 / Diagnostic value

A validated model becomes more useful for diagnostics

Once the model reproduces the reference injector behaviour with sufficient accuracy, individual parameters can be changed to investigate how specific physical changes influence the measurable current waveform.

This makes simulation particularly useful when experimental generation of every possible degradation state is difficult, expensive or time-consuming.

07 / My research perspective

Why I combine simulation and experiment

In my work, simulation is not intended to replace measurement. The two approaches answer different questions and become most useful when they are connected.

Experimental signals show how the real injector behaves. Mathematical and multidomain models help explain why the signal has a particular shape and how changes in physical parameters may influence it.

The goal is physical interpretation. A diagnostic feature becomes more meaningful when its relationship with injector behaviour can be explained, not only observed statistically.
