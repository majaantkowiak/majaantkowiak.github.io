Relative Injector Degradation Index

A mathematical framework for integrating multiple diagnostic symptoms into a single relative assessment of injector condition. The approach combines hydraulic, mechanical, electrical, electromagnetic and thermal degradation while preserving information about the contribution of each physical domain.

Condition Assessment Degradation Index Data Normalization Multidomain Diagnostics
01 / Model Assumptions

From multiple symptoms to one condition index

Injector degradation is treated as a multidimensional phenomenon. A single measured parameter cannot fully describe the condition of an electromagnetic injector, because hydraulic, mechanical, electrical, electromagnetic and thermal processes are mutually coupled.

The proposed quantity \(D_{TSI}\) is a relative degradation index defined within the interval \([0,1]\). A value close to zero represents the reference condition, while a value approaching one represents a strongly degraded or limiting condition.

MODEL RANGE
\[ D_{TSI}\in[0,1] \]

A complementary health index can be defined so that the interpretation is reversed: a value close to one corresponds to good technical condition.

EQ / 01
\[ HI_{TSI} = 1-D_{TSI} \]
Relative technical-condition index.
This model is not a probability-of-failure model expressed as a function of operating time. It is a diagnostic framework describing the relative level of degradation from deviations of measured parameters from their reference values.
02 / Symptom Normalization

Different measurements need a common scale

Diagnostic variables have different physical units, ranges and sensitivities. Before they can be aggregated, each measurement \(z_j\) is transformed into a dimensionless degradation symptom \(s_j\).

The reference value must be determined for defined operating conditions. For this reason, the reference value is treated as a function of the operating-state vector \(\chi\).

EQ / 02
\[ z_{j,0} = f_{j,0}(\chi) \]
EQ / 03
\[ \chi = \begin{bmatrix} p_{rail} & U & T & t_{inj} & p_{cyl} \end{bmatrix}^{T} \]
Operating conditions affecting the reference injector characteristic.

The normalized symptom is calculated from the deviation from the reference state. The parameter \(\varepsilon_j\) introduces a tolerance zone that accounts for measurement noise, manufacturing tolerance and natural cycle-to-cycle variability.

EQ / 04
\[ s_j = sat_{[0,1]} \left( \frac{ \left|z_j-z_{j,0}\right| - \varepsilon_j }{ \left|z_{j,lim}-z_{j,0}\right| - \varepsilon_j } \right) \]

The saturation function constrains every symptom to the same interval.

EQ / 05
\[ sat_{[0,1]}(x) = \begin{cases} 0, & x<0 \\[4pt] x, & 0\le x\le1 \\[4pt] 1, & x>1 \end{cases} \]
REFERENCE

\(s_j=0\)

No diagnostically significant deviation from the reference injector characteristic.

LIMITING CONDITION

\(s_j=1\)

The defined limiting deviation for the considered diagnostic variable has been reached or exceeded.

03 / Partial Degradation Indices

Symptoms are grouped by physical domain

The degradation model contains five physical groups: hydraulic \(H\), mechanical \(M\), electrical \(E\), electromagnetic \(EM\) and thermal \(T\).

EQ / 06
\[ g \in \left\{ H,M,E,EM,T \right\} \]

For each group, the available normalized symptoms form a symptom vector.

EQ / 07
\[ \mathbf{s}_g = \begin{bmatrix} s_{g,1} & s_{g,2} & \ldots & s_{g,n_g} \end{bmatrix}^{T} \]

The simplest partial degradation index is a weighted sum of the symptoms belonging to the considered group.

EQ / 08
\[ D_g = \sum_{j=1}^{n_g} a_{g,j}s_{g,j} \]
EQ / 09
\[ \sum_{j=1}^{n_g} a_{g,j} = 1, \qquad a_{g,j}\ge0 \]

If the index should react more strongly to one dominant symptom, the weighted power norm can be used.

EQ / 10
\[ D_g = \left( \sum_{j=1}^{n_g} a_{g,j} s_{g,j}^{p_g} \right)^{1/p_g} \]
\(p_g=1\)

Weighted sum

All symptoms contribute according to their defined weights.

\(p_g>1\)

Dominant symptom sensitivity

A strong individual symptom has a greater influence on the resulting partial index.

04 / Hydraulic Degradation

Hydraulic symptom vector

The hydraulic group combines information associated with fuel flow, injected mass, spray geometry, droplet size, particle-number emissions and end-of-injection fuel behaviour.

EQ / 11
\[ \mathbf{s}_H = \begin{bmatrix} s_{\dot m} & s_m & s_{\alpha} & s_{SMD} & s_{PN} & s_{dr} \end{bmatrix}^{T} \]
EQ / 12
\[ D_H = \alpha_{H,1}s_{\dot m} + \alpha_{H,2}s_m + \alpha_{H,3}s_{\alpha} + \alpha_{H,4}s_{SMD} + \alpha_{H,5}s_{PN} + \alpha_{H,6}s_{dr} \]
\(s_{\dot m}\)

Fuel flow

Reduction or deviation of fuel-flow rate.

\(s_m\)

Injected mass

Deviation of the delivered fuel dose.

\(s_{\alpha}\)

Spray angle

Change in spray geometry.

\(s_{SMD}\)

Droplet size

Increase in characteristic droplet diameter.

\(s_{PN},\,s_{dr}\)

Emission & dribbling

Particle-number response together with fuel dribbling or tip-wetting intensity.

05 / Mechanical Degradation

Mechanical symptom vector

EQ / 13
\[ \mathbf{s}_M = \begin{bmatrix} s_{t_{op}} & s_{t_{cl}} & s_x & s_{\sigma x} & s_b \end{bmatrix}^{T} \]
EQ / 14
\[ D_M = \alpha_{M,1}s_{t_{op}} + \alpha_{M,2}s_{t_{cl}} + \alpha_{M,3}s_x + \alpha_{M,4}s_{\sigma x} + \alpha_{M,5}s_b \]
\(s_{t_{op}}\)

Opening delay

Change in injector opening delay.

\(s_{t_{cl}}\)

Closing time

Change in injector closing dynamics.

\(s_x\)

Needle lift

Deviation of maximum needle displacement.

\(s_{\sigma x}\)

Cycle variability

Variability of needle motion between cycles.

\(s_b\)

Needle bounce

Intensity of needle or armature rebound.

06 / Electrical Degradation

Electrical symptom vector

EQ / 15
\[ \mathbf{s}_E = \begin{bmatrix} s_R & s_{I_p} & s_{I_h} & s_{\frac{di}{dt}} & s_{\sigma I} & s_{oc} & s_{sc} \end{bmatrix}^{T} \]
EQ / 16
\[ \begin{aligned} D_E ={}& \alpha_{E,1}s_R + \alpha_{E,2}s_{I_p} + \alpha_{E,3}s_{I_h} + \alpha_{E,4}s_{\frac{di}{dt}} \\[3pt] & + \alpha_{E,5}s_{\sigma I} + \alpha_{E,6}s_{oc} + \alpha_{E,7}s_{sc} \end{aligned} \]
\(s_R\)

Resistance

Change in coil or connection resistance.

\(s_{I_p},\,s_{I_h}\)

Peak & Hold current

Deviations of characteristic current levels.

\(s_{\frac{di}{dt}}\)

Current slope

Change in current build-up dynamics.

\(s_{\sigma I}\)

Repeatability

Cycle-to-cycle current instability.

\(s_{oc},\,s_{sc}\)

Circuit faults

Open-circuit and short-circuit symptoms.

07 / Electromagnetic Degradation

Electromagnetic symptom vector

EQ / 17
\[ \mathbf{s}_{EM} = \begin{bmatrix} s_L & s_{U_{ind}} & s_{\tau_m} & s_{F_{em}} & s_g \end{bmatrix}^{T} \]
EQ / 18
\[ D_{EM} = \alpha_{EM,1}s_L + \alpha_{EM,2}s_{U_{ind}} + \alpha_{EM,3}s_{\tau_m} + \alpha_{EM,4}s_{F_{em}} + \alpha_{EM,5}s_g \]
\(s_L\)

Inductance

Deviation of effective coil inductance.

\(s_{U_{ind}}\)

Induced voltage

Change in the characteristic induced-voltage transient.

\(s_{\tau_m}\)

Magnetic dynamics

Change in magnetic-field build-up or decay.

\(s_{F_{em}}\)

Electromagnetic force

Change in estimated electromagnetic force.

\(s_g\)

Magnetic gap

Change in magnetic gap or armature position.

08 / Thermal Degradation

Thermal symptom vector

EQ / 19
\[ \mathbf{s}_T = \begin{bmatrix} s_T & s_{R_T} & s_{ins} & s_{tip} \end{bmatrix}^{T} \]
EQ / 20
\[ D_T = \alpha_{T,1}s_T + \alpha_{T,2}s_{R_T} + \alpha_{T,3}s_{ins} + \alpha_{T,4}s_{tip} \]
\(s_T\)

Temperature

Increase in coil or core temperature.

\(s_{R_T}\)

Permanent resistance change

Resistance deviation remaining after cooling.

\(s_{ins}\)

Insulation condition

Deterioration of winding insulation.

\(s_{tip}\)

Injector-tip condition

Thermal influence on tip wetting and deposit formation.

09 / Overall Degradation Index

Five physical domains. One relative index.

Once all partial degradation indices have been calculated, they are combined into the overall injector degradation index.

EQ / 21
\[ D_{TSI} = w_H D_H + w_M D_M + w_E D_E + w_{EM}D_{EM} + w_TD_T \]
EQ / 22
\[ w_H+w_M+w_E+w_{EM}+w_T = 1 \]

The group weights define the relative importance of each degradation domain. Their final values require calibration against experimental data and can depend on the diagnostic objective.

10 / Diagnostic Confidence

Not every measurement carries the same information

Measurement availability and quality can differ between degradation domains. A confidence coefficient \(c_g\in[0,1]\) can therefore be introduced to reduce the influence of groups supported by incomplete, noisy or indirect diagnostic information.

EQ / 23
\[ w_g = \frac{ c_g\beta_g }{ \displaystyle \sum_{ k\in \{H,M,E,EM,T\} } c_k\beta_k } \]
Confidence-adjusted normalized group weight.
\(\beta_g\)

Base importance

Represents the assumed diagnostic importance of a degradation group.

\(c_g\)

Information confidence

Represents the quality or availability of diagnostic information for that group.

11 / Nonlinear Aggregation

Increasing sensitivity to a dominant degradation mode

A linear weighted sum may distribute the influence of degradation across all groups. When a single severe degradation mode should have a stronger influence on the final assessment, the overall index can be written using a weighted power norm.

EQ / 24
\[ D_{TSI} = \left( w_HD_H^p + w_MD_M^p + w_ED_E^p + w_{EM}D_{EM}^p + w_TD_T^p \right)^{1/p} \]
\(p=1\)

Linear aggregation

The equation reduces to the classical weighted sum of partial indices.

\(p>1\)

Dominant-domain sensitivity

A strongly degraded individual subsystem has a greater influence on the final index.

12 / Diagnostic Interpretation

Preserve the cause while simplifying the diagnosis

The mathematical framework supports diagnosis on two levels. Partial indices indicate which physical degradation domain dominates, while the overall \(D_{TSI}\) value provides one compact numerical assessment of injector condition.

CONDITION RELATION
\[ HI_{TSI} = 1-D_{TSI} \]
LEVEL 01

Partial diagnosis

\(D_H\), \(D_M\), \(D_E\), \(D_{EM}\) and \(D_T\) identify which degradation domain contributes most strongly.

LEVEL 02

Overall condition

\(D_{TSI}\) reduces multidimensional diagnostic information to one relative condition indicator.

The model should be treated as a mathematical diagnostic framework requiring experimental calibration. It does not replace physical analysis of individual degradation mechanisms.
