Project 01 · Multidomain Simulation

Injector Multidomain Simulation

A coupled simulation of an electromagnetic direct-injection fuel injector developed in MATLAB/Simulink and Simscape. The model integrates electrical, electromagnetic, mechanical, hydraulic and thermal phenomena within one simulation environment.
MATLAB / Simulink Simscape Multidomain Modelling
01 / Overview

One injector. Several physical domains.

The project was developed to reproduce the dynamic behaviour of an electromagnetic fuel injector while preserving the physical relationships between its main subsystems. Instead of describing the solenoid as an isolated electrical load, the simulation couples coil current and electromagnetic force with needle motion, fuel-flow conditions and thermal effects. This allows changes introduced in one physical domain to affect quantities observed in the others, including the electrical current waveform used in diagnostic analysis.

02 / Model Architecture

A coupled simulation environment

The complete Simulink/Simscape structure integrates the main processes governing electromagnetic injector operation within one model.

Multidomain injector model developed in MATLAB/Simulink and Simscape
03 / Coupled Physical Domains

From electrical excitation to fuel flow

Separate physical subsystems describe the key stages of injector operation while exchanging variables required to reproduce the complete transient response.

01 / ELECTRICAL

Solenoid electrical response

Describes injector current, coil resistance, position-dependent inductance, thermal effects and the control signal applied to the solenoid.

02 / MECHANICAL

Needle dynamics

Combines electromagnetic actuation with spring, damping and fuel forces to reproduce needle displacement during opening and closing.

03 / HYDRAULIC

Fuel-flow response

Links needle opening with the hydraulic fuel path and enables analysis of flow behaviour and injected fuel mass.

04 / Dynamic Response

Electrical signal and needle motion

The model provides access not only to the measurable electrical response but also to internal mechanical variables that are difficult to observe directly during injector operation.

Injector current

Simulated current waveform reproducing the characteristic Peak & Hold operating strategy and the transition between its individual phases.

Needle displacement

Simulated injector needle motion during a complete injection event, linking the electrical response with mechanical opening and closing behaviour.

05 / Model Validation

From simulation to measurement

Validation is based on comparison between the simulated injector current and a waveform obtained from laboratory measurements. Particular attention is paid to the current rise, Peak region, transition toward Hold, steady Hold behaviour and switch-off response. Similarity between the characteristic stages of both signals provides a basis for further use of the model in diagnostic studies.

Comparison of the measured injector current waveform and model output
06 / Parameter Sensitivity

How parameter changes reshape the signal

Effect of coil resistance variation on the injector current waveform

Electrical degradation becomes visible in the waveform

Changing the coil resistance modifies the electrical response generated by the same injector model. This sensitivity is important from a diagnostic perspective because degradation of electrical parameters can therefore produce measurable changes in the current signal.

Research Relevance

A simulation model built for diagnostic research

The model provides a controlled environment for investigating relationships between injector parameters, internal physical behaviour and the electrical waveform available for measurement. By combining multidomain simulation with laboratory validation, it can be used to study how selected degradations or parameter changes may manifest themselves in current-based diagnostic indicators.
