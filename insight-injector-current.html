Can Injector Current Reveal Mechanical Degradation?

The injector current waveform is often treated mainly as an electrical signal. However, in an electromagnetic injector, electrical, magnetic and mechanical processes are strongly coupled. This means that changes in needle motion or electromagnetic conditions may also influence the measured current waveform.

01 / Injection event

What happens during one injection?

During the peak phase, current rises rapidly to generate enough electromagnetic force to move the injector needle. Once the injector opens, the control strategy reduces the current to the hold level. During switch-off, the magnetic field collapses and the needle returns to its closed position.

PEAK

Rapid current increase to generate opening force.

TRANSITION

Opening phase between the initial peak and steady hold.

HOLD

Lower current level used to keep the injector open.

SWITCH-OFF

Current decays and the needle returns to the closed state.

Key idea: even though we measure electrical current, the waveform reflects a physical event that also includes needle motion and magnetic changes inside the injector.

02 / Coupling

Where does the mechanical information come from?

Needle motion changes the geometry of the magnetic circuit. As a result, the coil inductance may change during injector opening and closing. Because current dynamics depend on resistance and inductance, mechanical motion can leave characteristic features in the electrical waveform.

This is why current analysis may reveal more than only electrical behaviour. It can also provide indirect information about mechanical and electromagnetic processes inside the injector.

03 / Degradation

What could degradation change?

Changes in injector condition may influence the timing, shape and repeatability of the current waveform. These effects are not necessarily unique diagnostic signatures. Similar waveform changes may result from electrical, electromagnetic or mechanical causes, which is why several signal features should be analysed together.

Needle sticking

May delay or modify the characteristic opening transition.

Return spring degradation

May influence both opening and closing dynamics.

Increased friction

May change the timing and repeatability of injector movement.

A useful diagnostic method should therefore combine multiple signal features instead of relying on only one point or one threshold.

04 / Features

Which current features are useful?

Instead of analysing only the maximum current value, a diagnostic approach can use a set of time-domain and frequency-domain features describing different parts of the injection event.

Different features may carry information about different physical mechanisms. That is why feature selection is a central part of signal-based injector diagnostics.

05 / Diagnostics

Why does this matter for diagnostics?

The main advantage of current-based diagnostics is that the electrical signal can be measured without direct access to the injector needle. If relationships between waveform features and physical injector behaviour are identified correctly, current analysis may provide a non-invasive way to detect changes in injector condition.

In practical terms, this can support earlier detection of abnormal behaviour than a simple logic based only on fault codes or threshold events.

06 / My perspective

How this fits into my research

In my research, I use mathematical modelling, multidomain simulation and laboratory current measurements to investigate these relationships. The objective is not only to detect that a waveform has changed, but also to understand which physical processes may be responsible for that change.

This approach connects physical interpretation with signal processing, which is important for the development of reliable and meaningful diagnostic indicators.

Long-term goal: connect waveform features with injector condition, degradation mechanisms and reliability-oriented assessment.
