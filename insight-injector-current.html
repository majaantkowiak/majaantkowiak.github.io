Can Injector Current Reveal Mechanical Degradation?

The injector current waveform is often treated mainly as an electrical signal. However, in an electromagnetic injector, electrical, magnetic and mechanical processes are strongly coupled. This means that changes in needle motion or electromagnetic conditions may also influence the measured current waveform.

01 / Injection event

What happens during one injection?

During the peak phase, current rises rapidly to generate enough electromagnetic force to move the injector needle. Once the injector opens, the control strategy reduces the current to the hold level. During switch-off, the magnetic field collapses and the needle returns to its closed position.

PEAK

Rapid current increase used to generate opening force.

TRANSITION

Dynamic region between the current peak and steady hold.

HOLD

Reduced current used to maintain the injector open.

SWITCH-OFF

Current decays and the injector returns toward closing.

Key idea: even though current is an electrical quantity, the waveform represents a complete electromechanical event.

02 / Coupling

Where does the mechanical information come from?

Needle motion changes the geometry of the magnetic circuit. As a result, coil inductance may change during injector opening and closing. Because current dynamics depend on resistance and inductance, mechanical motion can leave characteristic features in the electrical waveform.

This is why current analysis may provide indirect information about mechanical and electromagnetic processes occurring inside the injector.

03 / Degradation

What could degradation change?

Changes in injector condition may influence the timing, shape and repeatability of the current waveform. These effects are not necessarily unique diagnostic signatures. Similar waveform changes may result from electrical, electromagnetic or mechanical causes.

Needle sticking

May delay or modify the characteristic opening transition.

Return spring degradation

May influence opening and closing dynamics.

Increased friction

May change timing and repeatability of injector movement.

A useful diagnostic method should combine multiple signal features instead of relying on one isolated parameter.

04 / Features

Which current features are useful?

Instead of analysing only the maximum current value, a diagnostic approach can use a set of time-domain and frequency-domain features describing different parts of the injection event.

Different features may carry information about different physical mechanisms. Feature selection is therefore a central part of signal-based injector diagnostics.

05 / Diagnostics

Why does this matter for diagnostics?

The main advantage of current-based diagnostics is that the electrical signal can be measured without direct access to the injector needle. If relationships between waveform features and physical injector behaviour are identified correctly, current analysis may provide a non-invasive way to detect changes in injector condition.

In practical terms, this approach may provide information about component behaviour before a simple threshold-based diagnostic decision is reached.

06 / My perspective

How this fits into my research

In my research, I use mathematical modelling, multidomain simulation and laboratory current measurements to investigate these relationships. The objective is not only to detect that a waveform has changed, but also to understand which physical processes may be responsible for that change.

This approach connects physical interpretation with signal processing and supports the development of meaningful diagnostic indicators.

Long-term goal: connect waveform features with injector condition and degradation mechanisms.
