Analytical Model of Injector Current & Voltage

An analytical description of the electrical behaviour of an electromagnetic fuel injector during the Peak, Hold and switch-off stages. The model begins with an equivalent RL representation and is then extended to describe the change of electrical behaviour associated with injector needle movement.

RL Model Peak & Hold Current Voltage Injector Dynamics
01 / Equivalent RL Model

Electrical description of the injector coil

The electromagnetic injector is first represented as a series RL circuit. The coil resistance is represented by \(R\), while \(L\) describes the effective inductance of the electromagnetic system. According to Kirchhoff's voltage law, the applied voltage is divided between the resistive voltage drop and the inductive voltage component.

EQ / 01
\[ U(t) = L\frac{dI(t)}{dt} + R I(t) \]
Basic equation of the equivalent RL injector model.
RESISTIVE TERM

\(R I(t)\)

Voltage drop resulting from the electrical resistance of the injector coil.

INDUCTIVE TERM

\(L\,dI/dt\)

Voltage associated with the rate of change of current and the magnetic energy stored in the coil.

Before the control pulse begins, no current flows through the injector coil. The initial condition is therefore

INITIAL CONDITION
\[ I(0)=0 \]
\(U(t)\)
coil voltage
\(I(t)\)
coil current
\(R\)
coil resistance
\(L\)
effective inductance
\(t\)
time
\(dI/dt\)
current rate of change
02 / Peak Phase

Current build-up after the voltage step

At the beginning of the injection event, the control system applies a high excitation voltage \(U_{peak}\). Due to the inductance of the coil, the current cannot rise instantaneously and instead follows the characteristic first-order response of an RL circuit.

EQ / 02
\[ I(t) = \frac{U_{peak}}{R} \left( 1-e^{-t/\tau} \right) \]

The rate of current build-up is determined by the electrical time constant

EQ / 03
\[ \tau = \frac{L}{R} \]

If the Peak excitation were maintained for sufficiently long, the current would approach the theoretical steady-state value

PEAK LIMIT
\[ I_{\infty} = \frac{U_{peak}}{R} \]
03 / Needle Motion

Two electrical stages of the Peak phase

During the first part of the Peak phase, the injector needle remains in its initial position. The effective inductance is denoted by \(L_I\), which gives the phase-specific time constant \(\tau_I\).

EQ / 04
\[ I_I(t) = \frac{U_{peak}}{R} \left( 1-e^{-t/\tau_I} \right), \qquad \tau_I = \frac{L_I}{R} \]
Stage I — injector needle before significant movement.

When the electromagnetic force becomes sufficient to initiate needle movement, the magnetic geometry of the injector changes. In the simplified two-stage representation, the second operating region is described by a different effective inductance \(L_{II}\).

Because current through an inductive element remains continuous, the second stage must begin from the current \(I_m\) already present at the instant \(t_m\) when movement begins.

EQ / 05
\[ I_{II}(t) = \frac{U_{peak}}{R} + \left( I_m- \frac{U_{peak}}{R} \right) e^{-\frac{R}{L_{II}} (t-t_m)} \]
Stage II — current response after the beginning of needle movement, with \(I_m=I(t_m)\).
The original two-stage idea is preserved here, but the second-stage expression is written with the correct initial current \(I_m\). Starting Stage II again from zero current would introduce an artificial discontinuity into the analytical waveform.
04 / Peak-to-Hold Transition

Current decay toward the Hold level

After the Peak interval, the high excitation level is reduced. At the transition instant \(t_{peak}\), the current is equal to \(I_{peak}\).

EQ / 06
\[ I(t_{peak}) = I_{peak} \]

For the simplified RL representation with an effective Hold voltage \(U_{hold}\), the current moves from its Peak value toward the new steady-state level according to

EQ / 07
\[ I(t) = I_{peak} e^{-\frac{R}{L_{II}} (t-t_{peak})} + \frac{U_{hold}}{R} \left( 1- e^{-\frac{R}{L_{II}} (t-t_{peak})} \right) \]
Peak-to-Hold current transition.
\(U_{peak}\) → \(I_{peak}\) → Current decay → Hold level
05 / Hold Phase

Maintaining the injector in the open state

During the Hold phase, the high current required for rapid opening is no longer necessary. A lower current level is sufficient to maintain the magnetic force required to keep the injector open.

In the idealized steady-state RL representation, the current derivative tends toward zero. Consequently, the inductive voltage component disappears from the averaged model and Ohm's law gives

EQ / 08
\[ I_{hold} = \frac{U_{hold}}{R} \]
Idealized steady Hold current.
FUNCTION

Lower holding current

The current is reduced after injector opening while maintaining sufficient electromagnetic force.

REAL SIGNAL

Switching ripple

In a practical controlled driver, the measured Hold current can oscillate around its target value due to switching regulation.

06 / Switch-Off

Current decay after the control signal ends

At the end of the injection event, the excitation is removed. Because energy is stored in the magnetic field, the current cannot fall instantaneously to zero.

For the idealized natural response of the RL discharge path, the governing equation becomes

EQ / 09
\[ 0 = L_{II} \frac{dI}{dt} + R I(t) \]
EQ / 10
\[ \frac{dI}{dt} = - \frac{R}{L_{II}} I(t) \]

If \(I_{off}\) denotes the current at \(t=t_{off}\), the current decay is

EQ / 11
\[ I(t) = I_{off} e^{ -\frac{R}{L_{II}} (t-t_{off}) } \]
Idealized exponential current decay.
The equation above represents an idealized RL discharge path. A real injector driver contains a flyback or clamping path, so the actual decay rate and terminal voltage also depend on the power-electronic circuit.
07 / Voltage Response

Voltage during Peak, Hold and switch-off

Before the injector control signal begins, the applied control voltage is zero.

EQ / 12
\[ U_0(t)=0 \]

During the Peak phase, the simplified model assumes that the high excitation voltage \(U_{peak}\) is applied to the RL circuit.

PEAK
\[ U_{peak} = L\frac{dI}{dt} + R I(t) \]

As the current increases, the proportions of the resistive and inductive voltage components change, but their sum remains equal to the externally applied Peak voltage.

In the simplified averaged Hold representation, the effective voltage is reduced to \(U_{hold}\).

HOLD
\[ U_{hold} = L_{II} \frac{dI}{dt} + R I(t) \]

When the Hold current reaches its idealized steady state, \(dI/dt=0\), hence

HOLD STEADY STATE
\[ U_{hold} = R I_{hold} \]

During switch-off, the current decreases rapidly. Using the passive sign convention for the inductive element,

INDUCTIVE VOLTAGE
\[ U_L(t) = L_{II} \frac{dI}{dt} \]

Because \(dI/dt<0\) during current decay, \(U_L\) reverses polarity relative to the current-growth phase. For the ideal natural RL response described above,

NATURAL RL RESPONSE
\[ U_L(t) = -R I(t) \]
Therefore, \(U_R+U_L=0\) is the Kirchhoff balance of the ideal discharge loop. It must not be interpreted as saying that the voltage measured at the injector driver is zero. In a real circuit, the observed switch-off voltage is determined by the flyback or clamping topology and by the chosen voltage reference.
08 / Diagnostic Meaning

Connecting physical behaviour with waveform features

The analytical model provides a direct physical interpretation of characteristic regions of the measured injector current waveform. Electrical parameters and mechanical movement influence different parts of the response, which makes the current signal useful for diagnostic analysis.

01 / RESISTANCE

Coil resistance

Influences the time constant, current growth and theoretical steady-state current.

02 / INDUCTANCE

Electromagnetic state

Changes in effective inductance modify transient current behaviour.

03 / NEEDLE MOTION

Mechanical response

Needle movement changes the magnetic conditions of the injector and can alter the local current slope.

04 / CONTROL

Peak & Hold strategy

The driver determines the characteristic Peak, transition, Hold and switch-off regions of the waveform.

Research Context

Theoretical basis for signal-based diagnostics

This simplified analytical description complements the multidomain numerical model. Its purpose is to make the relationships between electrical parameters, injector movement and characteristic waveform regions transparent before more complex coupled simulations and diagnostic analyses are used.
