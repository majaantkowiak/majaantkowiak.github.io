Analytical Model of Injector Current

An analytical description of the electrical current response of an electromagnetic fuel injector during the characteristic Peak, transition, Hold and switch-off stages. The model begins with an equivalent RL representation and is used to interpret changes observed in the injector current waveform.

RL Model Peak & Hold Current Response Injector Dynamics
01 / Equivalent RL Model

Electrical description of the injector coil

The electromagnetic injector is first represented as a series RL circuit. The coil resistance is represented by \(R\), while \(L\) describes the effective inductance of the electromagnetic system. According to Kirchhoff's voltage law, the applied voltage is divided between the resistive voltage drop and the inductive voltage component.

EQ / 01
\[ U(t) = L\frac{dI(t)}{dt} + R I(t) \]
Basic equation of the equivalent RL injector model.
RESISTIVE TERM

\(R I(t)\)

Voltage drop resulting from the electrical resistance of the injector coil.

INDUCTIVE TERM

\(L\,dI/dt\)

Voltage associated with the rate of change of current in the injector coil.

Before the control pulse begins, no current flows through the injector coil. The initial condition is therefore

INITIAL CONDITION
\[ I(0)=0 \]
02 / Peak Phase

Current build-up after the voltage step

At the beginning of the injection event, the control system applies a high excitation voltage \(U_{peak}\). Due to the inductance of the coil, the current cannot rise instantaneously and instead follows the characteristic first-order response of an RL circuit.

EQ / 02
\[ I(t) = \frac{U_{peak}}{R} \left( 1-e^{-t/\tau} \right) \]

The rate of current build-up is determined by the electrical time constant

EQ / 03
\[ \tau = \frac{L}{R} \]

During the first part of this phase, the injector remains closed and its effective inductance can be represented by \(L_I\).

EQ / 04
\[ I_I(t) = \frac{U_{peak}}{R} \left( 1-e^{-t/\tau_I} \right), \qquad \tau_I = \frac{L_I}{R} \]
03 / Needle Motion

Change of electrical behaviour during injector opening

When the electromagnetic force becomes sufficient to initiate movement of the injector needle, the magnetic conditions of the system change. The second part of the Peak phase can therefore be represented using a different effective inductance \(L_{II}\).

In a continuous analytical description, the second stage starts from the current already present when needle movement begins.

EQ / 05
\[ I_{II}(t) = \frac{U_{peak}}{R} + \left( I_m- \frac{U_{peak}}{R} \right) e^{-\frac{R}{L_{II}} (t-t_m)} \]
Simplified second stage of the Peak phase, where \(I_m\) is the current at the beginning of needle movement.
04 / Peak-to-Hold Transition

Current decay toward the Hold level

After the Peak interval, the high excitation level is reduced. At the transition instant, the current is equal to \(I_{peak}\).

EQ / 06
\[ I(t_{peak}) = I_{peak} \]

For the simplified RL representation with an effective Hold voltage \(U_{hold}\), the current moves from its Peak value toward the lower steady-state level according to

EQ / 07
\[ I(t) = I_{peak} e^{-\frac{R}{L_{II}} (t-t_{peak})} + \frac{U_{hold}}{R} \left( 1- e^{-\frac{R}{L_{II}} (t-t_{peak})} \right) \]
Peak → Transition → Hold
05 / Hold Phase

Maintaining the injector in the open state

During the Hold phase, the high current required for rapid opening is no longer necessary. A lower current is sufficient to maintain the electromagnetic force needed to keep the injector open.

In the idealized steady-state RL representation, \(dI/dt\) approaches zero. The Hold current can therefore be expressed as

EQ / 08
\[ I_{hold} = \frac{U_{hold}}{R} \]
FUNCTION

Lower current level

The Hold phase maintains the injector in the open position after rapid opening.

REAL SIGNAL

Current ripple

In a practical driver, switching regulation can produce oscillation around the desired Hold current.

06 / Switch-Off

Current decay after the control signal ends

At the end of the injection event, the excitation is removed. The current cannot disappear instantaneously because energy remains stored in the magnetic field.

EQ / 09
\[ 0 = L_{II} \frac{dI}{dt} + R I(t) \]
EQ / 10
\[ \frac{dI}{dt} = - \frac{R}{L_{II}} I(t) \]

If \(I_{off}\) denotes the current at the moment when excitation is removed, the idealized current decay is

EQ / 11
\[ I(t) = I_{off} e^{ -\frac{R}{L_{II}} (t-t_{off}) } \]
07 / Diagnostic Meaning

Connecting physical behaviour with waveform features

The analytical model provides a physical interpretation of characteristic regions observed in the injector current waveform. Changes in electrical parameters and injector behaviour can influence different regions of the response, which makes the current signal useful for diagnostic analysis.

01 / RESISTANCE

Coil resistance

Influences the electrical time constant and the current level predicted by the simplified RL model.

02 / INDUCTANCE

Electromagnetic behaviour

Changes of effective inductance modify transient current behaviour.

03 / NEEDLE MOTION

Mechanical response

Injector movement changes the magnetic conditions and can influence the shape of the Peak region.

04 / CONTROL

Peak & Hold strategy

The driver determines the characteristic Peak, transition, Hold and switch-off regions.

Research Context

Theoretical basis for signal-based diagnostics

The analytical model complements the complete multidomain numerical model by providing a transparent physical interpretation of the characteristic injector current waveform.
