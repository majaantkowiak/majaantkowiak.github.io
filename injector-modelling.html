Analytical Model of Injector Current & Voltage

An analytical description of the electrical response of an electromagnetic fuel injector during the Peak, Hold and switch-off phases. The model uses an equivalent RL representation to connect electrical parameters with the characteristic current and voltage transients observed during injector operation.

Equivalent RL Model Peak & Hold Current Response Voltage Response
01 / Model Assumptions

Equivalent electrical representation

In its simplest analytical form, the electromagnetic injector coil can be represented as a series connection of resistance and inductance. Kirchhoff's voltage law then separates the applied voltage into a resistive voltage drop and an inductive component associated with the changing current.

EQ / 01
\[ U(t) = L\frac{dI(t)}{dt} + R I(t) \]
Basic voltage equation for the equivalent series RL representation.
RESISTIVE COMPONENT

\(R I(t)\)

Represents the instantaneous voltage drop caused by the electrical resistance of the injector coil.

INDUCTIVE COMPONENT

\(L\,dI/dt\)

Represents the voltage associated with a changing coil current and therefore with energy storage in the magnetic field.

Before the control pulse is applied, no current flows through the injector coil. The initial condition for the analytical model is therefore

INITIAL CONDITION
\[ I(0)=0 \]
\(U(t)\)
injector coil voltage
\(I(t)\)
injector coil current
\(R\)
coil resistance
\(L\)
effective coil inductance
\(t\)
time
\(dI/dt\)
rate of current change
02 / Current Build-Up

Peak phase and exponential current rise

At the beginning of an injection event, the driver applies a high excitation voltage \(U_{peak}\). Because the injector coil is inductive, the current cannot change instantaneously. Instead, it rises progressively toward the steady-state value determined by the applied voltage and coil resistance.

EQ / 02
\[ I(t) = \frac{U_{peak}}{R} \left( 1-e^{-t/\tau} \right) \]
Current response to a constant voltage step for an RL circuit with zero initial current.

The rate at which the current approaches its steady value is determined by the electrical time constant

EQ / 03
\[ \tau = \frac{L}{R} \]
STEADY LIMIT

\(U_{peak}/R\)

If the Peak voltage were maintained long enough, the current would asymptotically approach this value.

TIME CONSTANT

\(\tau=L/R\)

A change in resistance or inductance modifies the rate of current rise and therefore changes the waveform shape.

03 / Needle Motion & Inductance

The electrical response changes when the needle moves

Before mechanical movement begins, the injector can be approximated using an effective inductance \(L_I\). The corresponding current rise can be described using the same RL solution with a phase-specific time constant.

STAGE I
\[ I_I(t) = \frac{U_{peak}}{R} \left( 1-e^{-t/\tau_I} \right) \qquad \tau_I = \frac{L_I}{R} \]
Stage I — current rise before significant needle or armature displacement.

When the electromagnetic force becomes sufficient to initiate movement, the magnetic geometry changes. The coil inductance therefore becomes a function of needle position. A more complete electrical description must include not only the current derivative, but also the effect of mechanical displacement on magnetic flux linkage.

COUPLED FORM
\[ U(t) = R I + L(x)\frac{dI}{dt} + I\frac{dL(x)}{dx}\frac{dx}{dt} \]
Extended equation for a position-dependent inductance in the linear magnetic approximation.

For a simplified piecewise analytical description, the moving stage can still be represented using a second effective inductance \(L_{II}\). To maintain continuity of current at the instant \(t_m\) when movement begins, the response is written using the current \(I_m=I(t_m)\) as the initial condition.

STAGE II
\[ I_{II}(t) = \frac{U_{peak}}{R} + \left( I_m-\frac{U_{peak}}{R} \right) e^{-\frac{R}{L_{II}}(t-t_m)} \]
Stage II — simplified current response after the beginning of needle movement.
The analytical model does not require a universal assumption that \(L_{II}\) must always be smaller or larger than \(L_I\). The important quantity is the dependence \(L(x)\) and its variation during movement. A change in inductance modifies the local electrical time constant and the current slope.
04 / Peak-to-Hold Transition

Reducing current after rapid injector opening

The high-current Peak stage is required primarily for rapid actuation. Once the injector has opened, the driver reduces the electrical excitation to a lower Hold level. At the transition instant \(t_{peak}\), the current is equal to \(I_{peak}\).

EQ / 04
\[ I(t_{peak}) = I_{peak} \]

After switching from \(U_{peak}\) to the lower effective voltage \(U_{hold}\), the current cannot jump instantaneously. It decreases exponentially from \(I_{peak}\) toward the new steady-state level.

EQ / 05
\[ I(t) = \frac{U_{hold}}{R} + \left( I_{peak} - \frac{U_{hold}}{R} \right) e^{-\frac{R}{L_h}(t-t_{peak})} \]
Current decay after transition from Peak excitation to the Hold operating level.
High excitation → \(I_{peak}\) → Current decay → Hold level
05 / Hold Phase

Maintaining the injector at a lower current level

During the Hold phase, the objective is no longer to accelerate the needle but to maintain sufficient electromagnetic force to keep the injector open. Consequently, the required current can be significantly lower than during the Peak phase.

In the averaged analytical representation, when the current has reached a quasi-steady state, \(dI/dt\rightarrow0\). The inductive voltage component becomes small and the current is determined primarily by the effective Hold voltage and coil resistance.

EQ / 06
\[ I_{hold,\infty} = \frac{U_{hold}}{R} \]
Idealized steady-state Hold current for an averaged constant-voltage model.
PHYSICAL ROLE

Maintain needle position

The electromagnetic field is maintained at a level sufficient to counteract the closing forces acting on the injector.

REAL DRIVER

PWM current ripple

In a practical Peak & Hold driver, switching control produces a small current ripple around the desired Hold level rather than an ideally constant current.

06 / Switch-Off

Current decay and release of magnetic energy

At the end of the injection command, the driver removes the excitation. The coil current cannot disappear instantaneously because energy remains stored in the magnetic field. The current therefore continues to flow through the available discharge or flyback path.

In the simplest natural RL decay model, where the source voltage is removed and the remaining electrical path contains only \(R\) and \(L\), Kirchhoff's equation becomes

EQ / 07
\[ 0 = L_{off} \frac{dI}{dt} + R I(t) \]
EQ / 08
\[ \frac{dI}{dt} = - \frac{R}{L_{off}} I(t) \]

Using the current \(I_{off}\) flowing at the switch-off instant \(t_{off}\), the corresponding idealized decay is

EQ / 09
\[ I(t) = I_{off} e^{-\frac{R}{L_{off}} (t-t_{off})} \]
Idealized exponential decay of current after the excitation source is removed.

The magnetic field contains energy immediately before switch-off. This energy must either be dissipated or transferred through the driver protection and recovery circuit.

MAGNETIC ENERGY
\[ E_L = \frac{1}{2} L I^2 \]
In a real injector driver, “switch-off” does not necessarily mean that the voltage measured directly across the coil instantly becomes zero. A flyback, clamp or energy-recovery circuit provides a path for the remaining current and can generate a large voltage excursion while the magnetic field collapses.
07 / Voltage Response

Peak, Hold and switch-off voltage behaviour

The current waveform is only one part of the electrical response. The voltage waveform reflects the control strategy applied by the injector driver and the inductive behaviour of the coil during rapid changes of current.

OFF → PEAK → TRANSITION → HOLD → SWITCH-OFF

Peak voltage

During the idealized Peak interval, the driver applies \(U_{peak}\). The resistive and inductive contributions redistribute during the current rise, but their sum remains equal to the applied voltage.

PEAK
\[ U_{peak} = R I(t) + L\frac{dI}{dt} \]

Hold voltage

After the Peak stage, the effective electrical excitation is reduced. In the simplified averaged representation, the Hold phase can be described by

HOLD
\[ U_{hold} = R I(t) + L_h\frac{dI}{dt} \]

When a steady Hold condition is approached, \(dI/dt\rightarrow0\), and the applied effective voltage mainly compensates the resistive voltage drop.

HOLD STEADY STATE
\[ U_{hold} \approx R I_{hold} \]

Switch-off voltage

During rapid current decay, the inductive component becomes dominant. With the voltage polarity defined consistently with the current direction, the inductive contribution is

FLYBACK
\[ U_L = L\frac{dI}{dt} \]
Since \(dI/dt<0\) during rapid current decay, the induced voltage reverses polarity relative to the excitation stage.
It is useful to distinguish between the driver command voltage and the actual voltage measured across the coil. The command may be switched off while the coil terminal voltage exhibits a substantial transient caused by self-induction and the driver clamp circuit.
08 / Diagnostic Interpretation

Why the analytical model matters

The analytical model provides a direct physical interpretation of characteristic regions observed in measured injector current and voltage waveforms. Changes in electrical parameters, electromagnetic conditions and mechanical motion can modify different parts of the signal, making the waveform a useful source of diagnostic information.

01 / RESISTANCE

Coil resistance

Affects the electrical time constant, current rise and the steady current level predicted by the simplified RL model.

02 / INDUCTANCE

Magnetic state

Changes in effective inductance alter transient current behaviour and connect the electrical waveform with the magnetic geometry of the injector.

03 / NEEDLE MOTION

Mechanical movement

Needle or armature displacement changes the magnetic circuit and can introduce characteristic changes in current slope.

04 / DRIVER

Control strategy

Peak voltage, Hold control and flyback behaviour determine the major electrical phases visible in the measured waveform.

Research Context

A theoretical basis for signal-based diagnostics

The analytical description does not replace the complete multidomain injector simulation. Instead, it provides a transparent theoretical layer for understanding why characteristic current and voltage features appear and how they depend on physical injector parameters. This interpretation can then support the selection of diagnostic features used in measurement-based injector condition assessment.
